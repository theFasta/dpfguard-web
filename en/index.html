Why DPF Guard

Short trips and mostly urban driving make it hard for the particulate filter to regenerate — exactly the conditions most common in the city. DPF Guard shows you what's happening to your DPF while you drive, so you know when a slightly longer drive is all it takes, without a trip to the shop just to find out.

Parameters monitored

Read directly from the engine's control unit over OBD2, updated in real time as you drive.

Regeneration status

Instantly see whether the DPF is regenerating: active, when the ECU is cleaning the filter, or passive, with a hot DPF and falling soot.

Soot mass

How much soot has built up in the filter, with a bar that adapts to your car's regenerations: percentage and estimated km to the next one.

DPF temperature

Filter temperature while driving and during regeneration.

Differential pressure

A key indicator of how clogged the filter is.

Regeneration outcome

Soot left after the last regeneration and failed attempts, so you catch a cycle that didn't complete early.

Regeneration history

The last regeneration always in view under the DPF status, with a timer for the one in progress, and the list of those seen by the app, interrupted ones included.

Distance since last regeneration

Counted by the app from the vehicle odometer, starting from the first regeneration detected with the app connected.

Total km and more

Total vehicle mileage, speed, coolant temperature and battery voltage, in a row of compact cards.

For every car, free

Even if your engine isn't supported for DPF monitoring yet, DPF Guard reads the standard OBD2 data every car must provide. No purchase needed.

Engine data

RPM, speed, coolant and oil temperature, intake air, rail pressure, battery voltage and more: only the parameters your car declares as supported are shown.

Trouble codes

Check-engine light and stored, pending and permanent fault codes, with a description. For DPF codes, also what to do. Read only: no clearing that would hide a fault.

Planner: your car's deadlines

Service, oil, filters, timing belt, insurance, road tax, inspection: DPF Guard reminds you when they are due, for any car, even without an adapter.

By date, by km or both

Whichever comes first. Repeating reminders start again by themselves from the date and km of the job, and the history keeps track of what you did.

Morning notifications

A reminder when a deadline gets close and on the day it is due, even with the app closed. For the ones you can't forget: "Nag me until it's done".

Mileage from the car (Pro)

With the adapter connected the mileage updates by itself, if the car provides it; otherwise you type it in. With several cars, each one is recognised when you plug in the adapter.

How it works

1

Connect the adapter

Plug the adapter into the socket under the steering wheel and, the first time, pair it with your phone over Bluetooth.

2

Open the app

DPF Guard reconnects by itself to your last adapter and reads the data straight from the car, no workshop tools needed.

3

Drive informed

When the DPF starts regenerating, a clearly audible alarm sounds, even with the phone in your pocket and the screen off.

Which adapter

You need an OBD2 Bluetooth adapter, the kind sold online (look for ELM327). We tested it with vLinker and Vgate iCar; others usually work too, but some cheap models are less reliable.

Compatible engines

DPF monitoring works on the 1.5 dCi (Renault, Dacia, Nissan and Mercedes 180 with the 1.5), tested on Dacia Duster and Mercedes A 180 CDI, and on the Mercedes 2.1 CDI, tested on a 2016 GLC 250d. You need a car from 2008 or newer: if you see data in the app's Engine tab, your car works. You can also try the DPF part for free before buying. More diesel engines are on the way.

Your engine isn't supported yet? Become a tester
